# Zenex — Work Log

**Wednesday, 12 August 2026**

Four pieces of work today: cleaners can now manage their own services, the
instant-quote booking path was built, frontend testing was introduced, and five
bugs were found and fixed.

---

## 1. Cleaners can manage their own services and prices

**Why:** cleaners could only edit their title, hourly rate, bio and languages.
Their actual **services and prices were fixed** at whatever was originally set
up, so a real cleaner joining Zenex had no way to say what they offer or what
they charge. This blocked recruiting cleaners entirely.

**Delivered:**

- Endpoints for a cleaner to list, add, edit and delete their own services,
  scoped so one cleaner can never touch another's listing.
- Deleting a service **detaches it from past bookings** rather than blocking or
  erasing them, so earnings history stays intact.
- A new **Services tab** on the cleaner dashboard with inline add/edit, price,
  duration, description, and a delete confirmation.
- An empty state offering one-tap presets (Standard Clean, Deep Clean, Move
  In/Out with sensible durations and prices) so a new cleaner is bookable in
  seconds instead of facing a blank form.
- Any change immediately updates their public profile and search listing.

---

## 2. Instant quote and automatic cleaner matching

**Why:** Zenex worked like TaskRabbit — browse cleaners, compare, pick one. The
leading Canadian competitors (Hellamaid, Jiffy, Cleany) don't work that way:
you enter your home size, see an exact price in under a minute, and the platform
assigns a vetted cleaner. That is the conversion path Zenex was missing.

**Delivered:**

- A **pricing model** driving exact prices from bedrooms and bathrooms — a base
  price plus a per-bedroom and per-bathroom amount, with job duration scaling
  the same way. Fully configurable per service type.
- A public **quote endpoint** returning tax-inclusive prices for every service
  type at a given property size.
- **Merged availability** across all cleaners — a time is offered if *anyone*
  is free, so the customer picks a time rather than a person.
- An **auto-matcher** that assigns the best-rated verified cleaner who is
  genuinely free, double-checking both that they have no clashing job *and*
  that the slot falls inside their published working hours.
- Homepage **"See your price in 60 seconds"** widget showing three priced
  options for the entered home size.
- A streamlined **/book** page: pick a date, pick a time, enter the address,
  confirm — then a confirmation showing which cleaner was assigned.
- Three default pricing tiers seeded (Standard $89, Deep $169, Move In/Out
  $209, before per-room additions and tax).

**Security note:** the price override used by this flow is a **server-side only
parameter** — it is deliberately not part of the request body, because a client
that could send its own price could pay $1 for a $200 clean.

## 3. Frontend testing introduced

**Why:** the backend had tests; the frontend had none, and a real bug had just
slipped through in frontend-only code.

**Delivered:**

- Jest configured for the Next.js app, scoped to the pure logic where a silent
  bug does real damage.
- The duration parser **extracted out of a component** into its own module so
  it could be tested at all — being buried in a component is precisely why it
  shipped broken.
- **16 frontend tests** covering duration parsing (units, ranges, fractions,
  nonsense input, absurd values) and time formatting (correct timezone, the
  midnight-boundary case, invalid input).
- Backend suite re-verified: **28 tests passing**. Total now **44**.

## 4. Bugs found and fixed

| # | Bug | What would have happened |
| - | --- | ------------------------ |
| 1 | Durations like "90 mins" were read as **90 hours** | No bookable slots at all, with no explanation — the cleaner would assume Zenex was broken |
| 2 | New cleaners were created with **no working hours** | Every date showed "no openings" forever. This would have hit the first real cleaner you recruited |
| 3 | Cleaners with **no services still appeared in search** | Customers clicked through to profiles they couldn't book |
| 4 | **"Invalid Date"** could render on a booking card | Visible broken text to customers. Caught by a test on its first run |
| 5 | **No upper limit** on booking duration | A bad value could have blocked out a cleaner's entire calendar |

Bugs 1, 2 and 3 were only exposed *because* cleaners could now manage their own
services — they were latent until real data could vary.

**Also added:** red dashboard warnings for the two states that silently stop a
cleaner receiving bookings — no services listed, and no working hours set. Both
were previously invisible to them.
